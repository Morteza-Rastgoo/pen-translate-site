Choosing a translator

Point Translate vs Google Translate: on-screen text vs camera

Both translate. They point at different things. Google Translate points a camera at the real world. Point Translate points at the text that is already on your screen. Here is the honest difference, and which one fits your situation.

Short version: If the words are on a physical sign, menu, or page, use a camera translator like Google Translate. If the words are on your phone — in a browser, chat, PDF, game, or settings screen — Point Translate reads them in place, on-device, without you leaving the app.

The one difference that matters

Every "translate" app in the store is really one of two tools:

  • Camera translators — you point the camera at the real world (a sign, a menu, a document) and the app reads the photo. Google Translate, Apple Translate, PolyPal, Translator+ all work this way.
  • On-screen translators — you point at text that is already displayed on your phone, in whatever app you are using, and the translation appears right there. That is Point Translate.

The second category is much smaller, which is why a search for "point translate" usually surfaces camera apps. If you are reading a foreign-language article, chat, or PDF on your phone, a camera app makes you leave the app, take a photo, and wait — Point Translate just lets you point and read.

When to use which

Use Google Translate (or another camera app) when: the text is in the physical world — street signs, restaurant menus, printed documents, packaging. You need the camera.
Use Point Translate when: the text is already on your phone — a browser article, a chat message, a PDF or e-book, a game, an email, a settings page. You do not want to switch apps, copy text, or take a screenshot. You point, and it translates in place.

What about FingerTrans and the other "point" apps?

FingerTrans is an offline phrasebook: you point at a pre-made phrase to hear or show it. It is not a general translator for arbitrary text you encounter. Apps that advertise "point and translate" with a camera (PolyPal, Translator+, PointTranslate the camera PWA) are camera translators — they photograph the world. Point Translate is the on-screen one: it reads what is displayed, in any app, without a photo.

Privacy, the part most of them skip

Point Translate runs the translation entirely on your device. Nothing you point at is uploaded, stored, or shared — there is no account, no analytics, and no advertising. For on-screen text that is often private (a message, a document, a bank app), that matters more than an extra language or two.

Questions

Is Point Translate the same as Google Translate?

No. Google Translate is built around pointing your camera at the real world — a sign, a menu, a document — and it can also translate copied text. Point Translate is built around text that is already on your phone's screen: a browser tab, a chat, a PDF, a game, a settings page. You point at it with your finger or an S Pen and the translation appears in place. They solve different jobs.

When should I use Google Translate instead of Point Translate?

Use Google Translate when the text is in the physical world and you need to point a camera at it — street signs, restaurant menus, printed documents. Use Point Translate when the text is already displayed on your screen and you do not want to leave the app, copy anything, or take a photo.

Does Point Translate work inside other apps like a browser or a chat?

Yes, that is its main purpose. The translation lens floats on top of whatever app you are using — browsers, chat apps, PDF readers, e-books, email, games, settings — and translates the text you point at without switching away.

Is Point Translate better than Samsung's built-in screen translate or Circle to Search?

The built-in options are convenient but limited: they cover a small set of language pairs and depend on the vendor's engine and its settings. Point Translate offers 60+ languages in both directions, runs on-device and offline, and works the same on any Android 11+ device, with an S Pen hover option on supported Samsung hardware.
